Soil Moisture Retrieval Using Sentinel-1 SAR Data

Bardhaman District · SAR Backscatter Processing & In-situ Validation

Sentinel-1 SAR ESA SNAP ArcGIS Pro Soil Moisture Retrieval SAR Backscatter

Overview

This project focuses on retrieving and mapping soil moisture across Bardhaman district using Sentinel-1 Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data. The objective is to generate reliable soil moisture estimates by applying SAR backscatter processing, calibration, speckle filtering, terrain correction, and moisture retrieval algorithms within SNAP and ArcGIS environments.

A secondary goal is to assess the accuracy of the satellite-derived soil moisture by comparing it with available in-situ field observations, thereby evaluating the reliability and applicability of Sentinel-1 data for operational soil moisture monitoring in the region.

Data & Tools

Satellite Data
Sentinel-1 GRD — VV & VH polarization
Ancillary Data
SRTM DEM, in-situ soil moisture field observations
Processing Platform
ESA SNAP, ArcGIS Pro
Study Area
Paschim & Purba Bardhaman District, West Bengal

Methodology

Data Acquisition & Subsetting
Downloaded Sentinel-1 GRD products for the study period, applied orbit files, performed thermal noise removal, and subset to the Bardhaman district AOI.
SAR Pre-processing
Radiometric calibration to sigma-naught (σ°), Refined Lee speckle filtering to reduce noise, and Range-Doppler terrain correction using SRTM DEM for geometric accuracy.
Feature Extraction
Converted backscatter values to decibel (dB) scale, derived VV/VH polarization ratios, and integrated NDVI as an ancillary vegetation descriptor to account for canopy effects.
Soil Moisture Retrieval
Applied empirical soil moisture retrieval models relating SAR backscatter intensity to volumetric soil moisture content with ancillary data integration for improved accuracy.
Validation & Mapping
Collocated satellite-derived estimates with in-situ field measurements, performed statistical validation using RMSE and R², and produced final district-level soil moisture maps in ArcGIS.

Validation Metrics

Statistical Fit
RMSE & R²
Classification

Uncertainty
Error Maps
Bias Analysis
Systematic Offset

Key Deliverables

  • Calibrated and terrain-corrected Sentinel-1 backscatter rasters for Bardhaman district
  • Soil moisture distribution maps with spatial coverage across both Paschim and Purba Bardhaman
  • Comprehensive validation report comparing satellite-derived estimates with in-situ observations
  • SNAP Graph Builder XML workflows for reproducible processing pipeline

Challenges & Solutions

  • Sparse in-situ sampling network → Applied temporal matching techniques to maximize data colocation
  • Vegetation canopy signal interference → Utilized VH/VV ratio and integrated NDVI to minimize vegetation bias
  • Surface roughness variability → Incorporated auxiliary surface roughness parameters in the retrieval model
  • Model uncertainty propagation → Developed systematic error analysis framework for result reliability assessment

Advanced Flood Hazard Mapping & Socio-Environmental Impact Assessment

Kamrup Metropolitan · Google Earth Engine · Migration & Displacement Analysis

Google Earth Engine Sentinel-1 SAR Sentinel-2 NDWI / MNDWI Night-time Lights Population Displacement

Overview

This project develops an advanced flood hazard mapping framework for Kamrup Metropolitan district using Google Earth Engine (GEE). The study integrates multi-temporal Sentinel-1 SAR data to detect flood inundation and evaluates flood severity at high temporal resolution. Flood classification is refined by validating surface water signatures using Sentinel-2 reflectance indices such as NDWI, MNDWI, and SWIR-based water detection, ensuring improved accuracy of the inundation maps.

Beyond mapping, the project assesses long-term socio-environmental implications by analyzing population displacement and migration patterns over the past decade, relying entirely on satellite-driven indicators without field surveys.

Data Sources

SAR Data
Sentinel-1 C-band multi-temporal stack (VV & VH)
Optical Data
Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery for water index validation
Socio-economic
Census layers, settlement change datasets, nighttime lights (VIIRS/DMSP)
Platform
Google Earth Engine (JavaScript API), QGIS, ArcGIS

Processing Workflow

SAR Time Series Ingestion
Ingested Kamrup Metropolitan AOI in GEE, created multi-temporal Sentinel-1 image collection with speckle reduction (focal mean filter) for noise suppression.
Flood Inundation Detection
Applied threshold-based and change detection methods on SAR backscatter, comparing pre-flood baseline scenes against flood-period imagery to delineate inundated extents.
Optical Index Refinement
Computed NDWI, MNDWI, and SWIR-band thresholds from Sentinel-2 to mask false positives including cast shadows, built-up reflectance, and permanent water bodies.
Flood Severity Classification
Classified flood severity as transient or sustained flooding using inundation duration, recurrence frequency, and backscatter magnitude patterns across multiple events.
Socio-spatial Impact Analysis
Overlaid flood maps with census and settlement layers for exposure analysis. Analyzed nighttime light trends, built-up density shifts, and informal settlement growth to infer displacement and migration drivers.

Socio-Environmental Assessment Focus

  • How recurrent flooding influences population redistribution across Kamrup Metropolitan
  • Migration hotspots and probable routes toward safer, higher-elevation zones
  • Patterns of informal settlement growth in chronically flood-prone pockets
  • Changes in built-up density following repeated flood disturbance events
  • Correlation between nighttime light intensity decline and displacement risk zones

Key Outputs

  • Multi-temporal flood extent maps at daily-to-weekly temporal resolution
  • Flood severity classification maps distinguishing transient vs. sustained inundation
  • Population exposure statistics and community vulnerability hotspot maps
  • Migration and displacement indicator maps derived from satellite proxies
  • Complete, reproducible Google Earth Engine JavaScript processing scripts

Asset Mapping of Symbiosis Lavale Campus Using Drone Data

UAV Photogrammetry · Orthomosaic & DSM Generation · Institutional Infrastructure Inventory

UAV / Drone Imagery Drone2Map Photogrammetry ArcGIS Pro DSM / DTM 3D Modeling

Overview

This project involves creating a comprehensive asset map of the Symbiosis Lavale campus using high-resolution drone imagery. Drone2Map is used to process UAV data into orthomosaics, digital surface models (DSM), and 3D products. These datasets are integrated into ArcGIS to accurately identify, digitize, and map campus assets such as buildings, roads, vegetation, open spaces, and infrastructure facilities.

The project provides a detailed spatial inventory that supports campus planning, management, and decision-making, demonstrating the effectiveness of drone-based mapping for institutional asset management.

Technology Stack

Data Acquisition
UAV RGB imagery with nadir and oblique captures
Photogrammetric Processing
Drone2Map (Esri) / Pix4D
GIS Platform
ArcGIS Pro, ArcGIS Online
Primary Outputs
Orthomosaic, DSM, DTM, Dense Point Cloud

Workflow

Mission Planning & Data Capture
Designed UAV flight mission with full campus coverage and 70%+ forward/side overlap ratios for optimal Structure-from-Motion (SfM) reconstruction quality.
Photogrammetric Processing
Image alignment and tie-point generation → Dense point cloud construction → Orthomosaic generation at centimeter-level resolution → DSM and DTM extraction using ground filtering.
Asset Digitization in ArcGIS
Digitized campus assets directly on orthomosaic layer, including buildings, roads, pathways, vegetation zones, open spaces, and utility infrastructure. Extracted height attributes from DSM-DTM differencing for 3D characterization.
Inventory Creation & Publication
Compiled feature classes into a structured geodatabase with full attribute tables. Exported printable campus maps and published an interactive web map on ArcGIS Online for facility management use.

Key Deliverables

  • High-resolution campus orthomosaic at centimeter-level ground sampling distance
  • Digital Surface Model (DSM) and Digital Terrain Model (DTM) for 3D analysis
  • Comprehensive asset geodatabase with feature classes for all campus infrastructure categories
  • Detailed attribute inventory spreadsheet covering area, height, condition, and asset type
  • Interactive ArcGIS Online web map for ongoing campus facility management

Comparative Analysis of ML Algorithms for Land Cover Classification

Decision Tree · Random Forest · SVM · Performance Benchmarking Using Python

Python Scikit-learn Decision Tree Random Forest SVM LULC Classification

Overview

This project evaluates and compares the performance of multiple machine learning algorithms for Land Use/Land Cover (LULC) classification using Python. The study applies Decision Tree (DT), Random Forest (RF), and Support Vector Machine (SVM) algorithms to classify satellite imagery and identify spatial patterns of land cover change.

The goal is to determine which algorithm performs most accurately for change detection across different datasets. The project encompasses the full pipeline from preprocessing and feature extraction through model training, accuracy assessment, and visualization of classification outputs to support improved land resource management.

Experimental Design

Dataset Preparation
Prepared a balanced, labeled training dataset using stratified random sampling across all LULC classes to ensure representative class coverage and minimize spatial autocorrelation bias.
Preprocessing & Feature Engineering
Applied atmospheric correction and band stacking on satellite imagery. Computed spectral indices including NDVI, NDWI, and NDBI as additional classification features to improve separability between classes.
Model Training & Hyperparameter Tuning
Trained DT, RF, and SVM models using scikit-learn. Applied systematic hyperparameter optimization via k-fold cross-validation (GridSearchCV) to maximize generalization across classifiers.
Accuracy Assessment & Comparison
Generated LULC classified maps for each algorithm, conducted comprehensive accuracy assessment using overall accuracy, Kappa coefficient, per-class precision/recall/F1, and processing time benchmarks.

Forest Cover Change & Climatic Variability in the Western Ghats

Western Part of Western Ghats · Fire Dynamics · 2001–2022 Long-term Analysis

Landsat / Sentinel MODIS Fire Land Surface Temperature Rainfall Analysis Google Earth Engine Time Series

Overview

This project investigates how forest cover change in the western part of the Western Ghats is influenced by climatic variability. The study analyzes multi-temporal satellite imagery to assess forest loss, degradation, and land cover transitions, and correlates these changes with climatic factors such as rainfall patterns and land surface temperature (LST).

The research also incorporates wildfire dynamics as a key disturbance agent. Using long-term fire activity records (2001–2015), the project examines temporal and spatial patterns of wildland fires and evaluates how inter-annual climate variability contributes to fluctuations in burned area, revealing that years like 2000, 2013, and 2022 experienced nearly five times more fire activity compared to low-fire years.

Multi-source Data

Land Cover Imagery
Multi-temporal Landsat & Sentinel-2 for forest change mapping
Fire Data
MODIS Active Fire (MOD14A1) & Burned Area (MCD64A1) products
Climate Variables
ERA5 / IMD rainfall, MODIS LST (MOD11A2) time series
Analysis Platform
Google Earth Engine, Python (pandas, scipy)

Analytical Framework

Multi-temporal Land Cover Mapping
Compiled annual and seasonal land cover maps using supervised classification, harmonized across sensors to ensure temporal consistency of forest cover estimates.
Forest Change Detection
Detected forest loss and gain using post-classification comparison and CCDC (Continuous Change Detection and Classification) algorithms to identify abrupt and gradual transitions.
Fire Dynamics Integration
Integrated MODIS fire frequency and burned area records with forest change maps, identifying fire-driven deforestation hotspots and analyzing inter-annual burned area fluctuations.
Climate Attribution Analysis
Correlated forest loss and fire activity with climatic anomalies in LST and rainfall using lagged time-series regression, revealing weak-to-strong climate–fire relationships varying across landscape types.

Key Findings

  • Notable inter-annual variation in fire frequency: high-fire years (2000, 2013, 2022) recorded ~5× more activity than low-fire years
  • Climate–fire relationships ranged from weak to strong depending on landscape type and vegetation density
  • Extreme LST anomalies and rainfall deficits identified as primary drivers of elevated wildfire risk and subsequent forest cover decline
  • Hotspot zones of forest loss concentration mapped, revealing priority areas for conservation intervention

Outputs & Impact

  • Annual forest change maps (2001–2022) quantifying loss and gain extents
  • Time-series plots correlating forest cover dynamics with climate and fire variables
  • Spatial hotspot maps of critical forest loss zones and fire frequency clusters
  • Statistical report linking climate drivers to forest change at regional and landscape scales
  • Conservation planning insights for one of India's most sensitive biodiversity hotspots

Urban Encroachment Analysis in the Eastern Kolkata Wetlands

Ramsar Site · LULC Change Modeling · MOLUSCE Future Scenario Simulation

MOLUSCE Landsat / Sentinel QGIS / ArcGIS Cellular Automata Future Scenarios Wetland Conservation

Overview

This project investigates the patterns, drivers, and impacts of urban encroachment on the Eastern Kolkata Wetlands (EKW) — a Ramsar-designated wetland of international importance. Using multi-temporal satellite imagery and geospatial analysis tools, the study quantifies historical changes in land use and identifies the rate of wetland shrinkage over time.

MOLUSCE is employed to model land cover transitions and predict future scenarios of wetland loss and urban expansion under varying anthropogenic and climatic pressures, offering insights into sustainability challenges and supporting conservation and policy planning for the Eastern Kolkata Wetlands.

Methodology

Multi-temporal LULC Classification
Produced consistent, harmonized LULC maps for key years (2000, 2010, 2020) using supervised classification on Landsat and Sentinel imagery, ensuring temporal comparability across sensors.
Wetland Change Quantification
Computed area statistics and land cover transition matrices to quantify wetland loss pathways, identifying which classes are encroaching on EKW and at what rates over each decade.
Spatial Driver Analysis
Analyzed spatial drivers of encroachment including proximity to major roads, distance to urban centers, development corridor alignments, and population density gradients.
MOLUSCE Transition Modeling
Calibrated land cover transition models in MOLUSCE using artificial neural networks and cellular automata, integrating spatial drivers as explanatory variables for predictive accuracy.
Future Scenario Simulation
Simulated projected land cover scenarios for 2030 and 2040 under business-as-usual and alternative urban growth trajectories, quantifying potential wetland loss under each scenario.

Key Deliverables

  • Multi-temporal LULC change maps for 2000, 2010, and 2020 with quantified class transitions
  • Land cover transition matrices revealing historical encroachment pathways and rates
  • Future scenario maps for 2030 and 2040 under contrasting urban growth trajectories
  • Priority conservation area maps identifying the most at-risk wetland zones
  • Policy recommendations for sustainable urban planning around the EKW Ramsar site

Visualization of Land Subsidence Due to Coal Mining Using Sentinel-1

Paschim Bardhaman District · SAR Interferometry · Mining-induced Geohazard Assessment

Sentinel-1 InSAR ESA SNAP SAR Interferometry Deformation Analysis ArcGIS Pro Geohazard Mapping

Overview

This project focuses on detecting and visualizing land subsidence in the coal mining regions of Paschim Bardhaman district using Sentinel-1 SAR data. The study utilizes SAR interferometry and deformation analysis techniques to identify ground displacement caused by extensive underground mining activities in one of India's most coal-intensive districts.

By processing Sentinel-1 imagery — including calibration, filtering, and interferogram generation — the project produces detailed subsidence maps highlighting zones of significant vertical ground movement. ArcGIS is used to visualize and interpret spatial patterns, enabling better understanding of mining-induced geohazards and supporting regional risk assessment and planning.

Technical Infrastructure

SAR Data
Sentinel-1 IW SLC time series (C-band, ascending/descending)
Ancillary Data
High-resolution DEM, coal mining lease boundary shapefiles
Processing Software
ESA SNAP, interferometric processing tools
Visualization
ArcGIS Pro, Python (matplotlib, plotly) for displacement mapping

InSAR Processing Workflow

Data Acquisition & Stack Preparation
Collected Sentinel-1 SLC acquisitions covering the mining areas of Paschim Bardhaman, applying orbit correction and co-registration of the image stack for precise alignment.
Interferogram Generation
Generated differential interferograms from co-registered image pairs, isolating surface deformation phase by removing the flat-earth and topographic phase components using the DEM.
Phase Filtering & Coherence Assessment
Applied adaptive Goldstein phase filtering to enhance fringe visibility. Computed coherence maps to identify reliable scatterers and mask low-coherence vegetated regions.
Phase Unwrapping
Performed phase unwrapping using minimum cost flow algorithms to convert wrapped interferometric phase to continuous displacement measurements in the line-of-sight direction.
Displacement Estimation & Geocoding
Converted line-of-sight displacement to vertical ground motion estimates. Geocoded results to geographic coordinates and referenced to stable reference points outside mining zones.
Visualization & Risk Mapping
Generated subsidence magnitude maps in ArcGIS, overlaid with mining lease boundaries, road networks, and settlement layers. Produced hazard zonation maps identifying critical displacement zones for infrastructure risk assessment.

Visualization Outputs

  • Subsidence magnitude maps showing spatial extent and intensity of ground displacement across mining areas
  • Wrapped and unwrapped interferogram visualizations illustrating deformation fringes
  • Coherence maps identifying zones of reliable deformation measurement vs. noise
  • Displacement maps overlaid with coal mine lease boundaries for direct geohazard attribution
  • Infrastructure risk overlay identifying roads, buildings, and settlements in subsidence-prone zones
  • Hazard zonation map classifying areas by severity of mining-induced ground movement

Challenges & Solutions

  • Temporal decorrelation in agricultural areas → Focused analysis on coherent urban and bare-earth zones near mine boundaries
  • Atmospheric phase delays → Mitigated through careful scene pair selection and reference area normalization
  • Phase ambiguity in high-deformation zones → Applied robust unwrapping algorithms with coherence-weighted masking
  • Complex subsidence bowl geometry → Cross-sectional profiling and 3D terrain overlays for spatial interpretation
